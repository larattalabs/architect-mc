# Mosswater Stilts

## Mood
A village of damp timber that has outlived several of its builders. It leans, it sags and it is slowly being eaten by moss, but the lanterns are still lit and the nets are still drying.

## Silhouette
Every building stands on **mangrove log stilts** at least 3 blocks above the water or mud. Bodies are narrow and tall: 1–2 storeys of 4 blocks, with the upper storey jettied out 1 block. Ridges are steep and **crooked**: offset the ridge by one block, or step half the roof down a slab. Chimneys lean. From a distance the village should read as a forest of dark piles under top-heavy, kinked roofs.

## Roles
- **wall (spruce planks):** the main skin of every building.
- **wall_alt (stripped spruce log):** bleached, sun-worn boards. Patch it in irregularly on the seaward and weather sides, never as neat stripes.
- **trim (dark oak log):** corner posts, sills, jetty beams and door lintels.
- **frame (mangrove log):** stilts, cross-braces and boardwalk supports. It also sets the doors, fences and trapdoors to mangrove.
- **roof (dark oak planks):** stairs and slabs for every roof.
- **accent (moss block) / drape (moss carpet) / creeper (vine):** growth. Heavy at the waterline and the lower roof courses, thinning out as you go up.
- **foundation (mossy cobblestone):** only where the stilts meet the mud, as pile caps, and for the hall's base.
- **floor:** spruce planks. **path:** coarse dirt on the shore; on the water, use spruce slab boardwalks.
- **glass:** brown stained panes, small and grimy.

## Roof & windows
Gables at a pitch of 1, overhang 1, with stair ends left ragged. Add moss carpet patches on the eaves. Use single-pane windows every 3 blocks, set unevenly (skip one, drop another a block) and framed with trapdoor shutters. Don't use window bands.

## Components
- **stilt_pile:** a mangrove log with a mossy cobble cap at its base and fence cross-bracing between piles.
- **boardwalk:** a 2-wide spruce slab walk on mangrove posts, with mangrove fence rails on one side only. Let it dip a slab in the middle.
- **lantern_post:** a fence post with a fence arm and a hanging lantern. Put one at doors, corners and pier ends.
- **door_surround:** a dark oak log lintel, with a stair or slab step down to the boardwalk.
- **roof_trim:** an upside-down stair fascia along the eaves, with moss carpet on top.
- **chimney:** mossy cobble below the roof and leaning by one block above it, capped with a campfire.
- **net_loft / drying_rack:** fences, cobwebs and barrels under the overhangs, plus slab shelves holding barrels.
- **window:** described above.

## Tiers
**Humble** buildings (sheds, net huts, houses) use only spruce, stripped spruce, dark oak roofs and mangrove stilts. **Important** buildings (the fish hall, the harbour master's house, the lighthouse tower) earn dark oak trim framing, mossy cobble bases, extra lanterns, heavier vines and fence railings. They are bigger, but just as crooked.

## Do
- Offset, tilt or sag at least one element per building.
- Keep every building off the ground.
- Make moss densest near the water.
- Connect buildings with boardwalks.

## Don't
- Don't use straight, symmetrical facades.
- Don't use stone walls above the pile caps.
- Don't use bright or clean blocks, big glazing or flat roofs.
- Don't spread lighting evenly. Keep it in pools.
